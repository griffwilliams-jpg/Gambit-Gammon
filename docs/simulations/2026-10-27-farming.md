# Repeat-hit farming test under Economies B and C (27 October 2026)

**Status:** simulation result for discussion. It tests the main risk in the [development plan](../development-plan.md): that deliberately getting hit for Dinars might be stronger than playing for position. It covers one opponent policy, so it is evidence, not proof.

## What was run

- 1,000 match seeds (`farm-0` to `farm-999`). The farmer took white on even seeds and black on odd seeds, so seat and first-move effects cancel out.
- Each seed was played under Economy B (+1/+2, current) and C (+1/+3).
- The opponent was always the standard whole-turn planner. As a control, the planner also played itself on the same seeds and seats, which should come out at about 50%.
- The farmer uses the planner's whole-turn search with a different view of exposed checkers. There are four intensities:
  - **cautious:** each direct shot at its blot costs 1 point, against 3 for normal play;
  - **neutral:** shots cost nothing;
  - **eager:** each shot is worth the Dinars a hit pays (+2 under B, +3 under C);
  - **maximal:** each shot is worth double that.
- Farmers never shield their own blots with Flush or Malik.
- No game reached the 2,000-turn cap. Runtime was about 16 minutes for roughly 10,000 games.
- Reproduce with `npm run simulate -- --experiment farming --games 1000`.

## Findings

**No farming intensity beat the standard planner, under B or C.** The control won 48.7%. Cautious farming won 45.2% under B and 46.1% under C; about 3 points lower, and the intervals overlap with the control's. Neutral farming won about 43%, which is lower again. Eager and maximal farming collapsed to under 3%.

**Farming earns more money but loses the board.** The eager farmer earned about 49 Dinars a game under B against the planner's 37.5, and bought more lamps. It was still hit about 20 times a game, games ran longer, and it lost 97% of them. The +2 or +3 Dinars and the lamps they buy did not make up for the pips, re-entry tempo and cards lost to each hit.

**Economy C doesn't make farming pay.** Paired by seed, cautious farming did 0.9 percentage points better under C than B (interval −1.9 to +3.7), which is no clear difference.

**No runaway repeat-hit loops.** For the cautious farmer, the most hits on any one checker in a game averaged about 2.6, the same as its opponent. No game came near the turn cap.

**This is a favourable test for farming.** The planner opponent hits blots whenever that's good for its position, and never declines a hit to deny Dinars. So the farmer got paid as often as it could reasonably hope for, and it still lost.

## What this does and doesn't show

- It supports keeping Economy B as the default with no anti-farming rules for now, as the rules already direct.
- It doesn't test a farmer that spends its Dinars more cleverly (for example, saving for Enchanted or Royal lamps), an opponent that refuses hits to deny income, or human players who might value Jinn differently.
- The farmer's shop and power choices are the same as the planner's. Only its view of exposed blots changes.
- Win-rate intervals are Wilson 95% intervals. The other intervals are normal approximations.
